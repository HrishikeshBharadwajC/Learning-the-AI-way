I read papers for fun and build agents for work. I started out in classical machine learning, and for the last three years I've worked in generative AI: large language models, agentic systems, and whatever new model family shows up next.

Along the way I noticed that the most useful lessons rarely came from launch posts. They came from building things, watching them break, and reading closely to understand why.

This blog is where I write those lessons down, engineer to engineer.

## What you'll find here

- **Agentic systems.** How agents plan, call tools and fail, from building them for real.
- **Inside new models.** New architectures, training methods and model families, explained plainly.
- **Building with LLMs.** Prompts, retrieval and evaluation, and the failures that taught me the most.
- **Everyday AI.** How I use AI outside work to plan, write and learn faster.

## What's coming

These are the first posts I'm working on:

- What makes an agent reliable in production
- From classical ML to generative AI: what carried over
- System One models, explained
- Development practices in the age of coding agents

## How I'll write

Practical and specific. When I share a prompt or a design, I'll share the whole thing. When something didn't work, I'll say so.
